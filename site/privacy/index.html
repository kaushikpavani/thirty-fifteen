Privacy Policy

Effective November 16, 2026.

Who this covers

This policy describes the iPhone app 30/15 (bundle id com.kaushikpavani.thirtyfifteen), made by Sri Kaushik Pavani. It explains what the app stores on the phone and what can be sent to the app’s own backend.

That backend is hosted by Supabase. Supabase stores the database and runs sign-in for 30/15. Apple and Google are involved only when you choose Continue with Apple or Continue with Google.

Without an account

You can use 30/15 without signing in. The finished ride record, FTP, workout settings, saved sensor pairings, and any fitness details you enter (age, sex, weight, resting heart rate) stay on the iPhone. The ride clock, cues, and music do not need a network. The ride record itself is not uploaded until you sign in.

If this install can reach the backend, it may still send a short product event when you are not signed in. The events are: the app opened, a workout started, a workout finished, you signed in or out, or you sent a note. A workout event can include the FTP and interval lengths you were using, whether the ride was completed, and how long it ran. A sign-in event records Apple or Google as the provider. These events do not include your email or your name. The phone also sends an install id, the platform, and the app version so the event can be stored once. The events are used to see whether the app is opening and whether rides are finishing. They are not used for advertising, and they are not sold.

Settings → Leave a note sends the text you write, the platform, and the app version, with or without an account. If a name is already on the phone for you, that name is included. A note you have not sent yet stays in a queue on the phone until the app can reach the server.

If you sign in

Sign-in is optional. On iPhone, Settings → Account offers Continue with Apple and Continue with Google. Apple or Google, through Supabase Auth, gives the app a user id and an email address. Apple may supply a private relay address instead of your personal mailbox. The name the provider supplies, when there is one, is stored as the display name on your profile (the first word of that name).

When you are signed in and the phone is online, the app copies the following to your account on Supabase:

  • Finished workout summaries. Start and end time, duration, planned duration, FTP, the hard and easy target watts, whether you completed the plan, and a completion percent. If a power meter or heart-rate sensor was connected, the summary can also include averages and peaks for watts and heart rate, time in the hard and easy parts, a short power sparkline, and per-rep watts and heart rate. The summary does not include your name or email.
  • Profile. The display name, FTP, and the time the app was last opened while signed in.
  • Settings you use for the ride. Interval lengths, hard and easy targets, sound, haptics, music, coach voice and language, and the optional fitness fields age, sex, weight, and resting heart rate, plus the flags that remember FTP choices. A notice about the latest automatic FTP change on this phone is not part of that copy.
  • Product events while signed in. The same event names as above, stored with your user id. Event details still omit email and name.
  • Notes you send while signed in. The text, your user id, and the name on the account.

The phone remains the copy you ride from. If a sync fails, the ride is still on the iPhone and the upload is tried again later. Signing out stops new copies from being sent. Rides already on the phone stay there.

A note stored on the backend can be emailed to the developer through Resend, when that notification is turned on, so the note can be read. If you were signed in, your account email can be included on that message so a reply is possible. This page does not publish that inbox.

Bluetooth

Bluetooth is used only to read a power meter, smart trainer, or heart-rate sensor you choose. Power uses the Cycling Power service, or the Fitness Machine service when a trainer does not offer cycling power. Heart rate uses the Heart Rate service, including a watch that broadcasts heart rate. The permission text is: “30/15 reads cycling power and heart rate from your sensors.” iOS shows it when you open a connect screen, not at first launch.

Readings are used on the phone during the ride. They are not a location scan. The app does not request location permission. The name and id of the last sensor you paired stay on the phone so it can reconnect. Those sensor names are not a column on the workout summary. During a ride, Bluetooth may keep delivering power and heart rate while another app is in front.

What we do not collect

  • No ads, no advertising identifiers, and no sale of personal data.
  • No tracking across other companies’ apps or websites. The app’s privacy manifest sets tracking to off.
  • No HealthKit, and no reading of the Apple Health store.
  • No location, contacts, photos, or payment data.
  • No microphone recording. Spoken cues play out. They are not captured from you.
  • No connection to other training services. The app does not sign you into Strava, Garmin, or a similar service, and it does not import activities from them.

From a finished ride you can email a summary with the iPhone Mail sheet, or share a card through the system share sheet. That message leaves from your own account on the phone. 30/15 does not receive a copy of it.

How long it is kept

Data stays until you delete it with the controls below, or until you delete the account. Product events and notes that were sent with no user id are not attached to an account. Deleting an account later does not find those unsigned rows. Events and notes sent while you were signed in are removed with the account.

The connection to the backend uses HTTPS. A signed-in rider can read their own rides and settings. The database rejects reads of another rider’s rows.

Deleting your data

In the app, Settings → Your data:

  • Delete ride history removes rides from this iPhone. If you are signed in, it also deletes the account’s copies of rides that ended at or before that wipe.
  • Clear queued notes and analytics drops notes and product events that are still only on the phone.
  • Erase data on this iPhone removes rides, settings, and saved sensors from this phone, and asks the backend to delete this install’s device row.
  • Delete account and cloud data is shown while you are signed in. Delete account removes the cloud copy of your rides, profile, settings on that profile, product events tied to the account, and notes tied to the account, then deletes the sign-in account. Install rows are unlinked from the account. Rides on this iPhone stay unless you choose Delete account and iPhone data, which erases this phone as well.

Account deletion runs in the app’s delete-account function on Supabase. The function checks the signed-in session, deletes that account’s cloud rows, then deletes the auth user. If the phone cannot reach the server, the request stays queued on the phone and is tried again. You are signed out after the server accepts the delete. One ride can also be removed from Past rides (press and hold, or Delete this ride on the ride page) and, if you are signed in, from the account.

Children

30/15 is not directed at children under 13. The optional age field in the app starts at 13.

Not medical advice

FTP targets and any VO₂max figure in the app are training estimates from the numbers you enter and the sensors you pair. They are not medical advice, not a diagnosis, and not a measurement from a lab.

Changes

If this policy changes, the effective date at the top of this page will change. The current date is November 16, 2026.

Contact

Questions about this policy or about your data go to GitHub Issues: github.com/kaushikpavani/thirty-fifteen/issues. Please do not send personal data you do not want stored in that public issue.
